# Zusammenfassug
## 1. Introduction
Prostheses are used to replace missing body parts or help restore their function. Some replace parts of the arms or legs, while others repalce joints inside the body, such as the hip or knee.
This text explains arm and leg prostheses, and how modern technologies are improcing their performance. Artifical joints are discussed as an additional example of how prosthetic devices can restore movement.
## 2. What is a Prosthesis?
A prosthesis is different from an orhosis, which supports, protects or improves the function of an existing body part rather than replacing a missing one. Most limb prostheses consist of several important components. The socket is the part that connects the prosthesis to the remaining part of the person's limb, which is often called the residual limb. The socket must fit securly and comfortably because it transfers forces between the body and the artifical limb. Prostheses are often made from lightweight materials such as:
- Carbon fibre
- Titanium
- Aluminium
- Silicone
- Specialised plastics
## 3. Different Types of Arm Prostheses
Arm/upper-limb prostheses improve everyday activities such as eating, dressing, writing and working. There are several types of arm prostheses.
### Passive Prostheses
Mainly designed for appearance. The silicon can imitate the appearance of human skin. It can help maintain balance and is often lightweight and requires little maintencance.
### Body-Powered Prostheses
A person with a forearm prosthesis may move their sholder or upper arm to pull a cable. This movement activates the gripping mechanis, allowing the artificial hand or hook to open or close. They can feel some of the resistance created when the device grips an object, which can cause discomfort.
### Myoelectric Prostheses
They use electrical signals to produced by muscles to control an artifical hand, wrist or elbow. To operate sensores, usually electrodes placed agains the skin. For example, the user may contrac one muscle to close the artifical hand and another muscle to open it. They can be expensive and relatively heavy. They require batteris, regular maintenance and training. Sweat, changes skin contanc and muscle signals can also affect control. A myoelectric hand does not automatically function like a natrual human hand.
### Hybrid Prostheses
These combine different types. For example, an artificial elbow might be controlled by a body-powered cable, while an artificial hand is controlled by electrical signals from the muscle.
### Specialised Arm and Hand Prostheses
Some Prostheses are designed for particular activities rather than general everday use. Some of these activities are for cycling, swimming, weightlifting, playing musical instruments or using specialised tools.
## 4. Different Types of Leg Prostheses
Leg/lower-limb prostheses improve everyday activities such as standing, walking and running. There are several types of leg prostheses, which deoends largly on the level at which the limb is missing.
### Foot and Partial-Foot Prostheses
They may help distribut pressure across the foor and provide support during walking. Some prostetic feet store energy and releases it during the next phase of walking. This can make movement more efficient.
### Below-Knee Prostheses
The medical term for this level of amputation is trantibial amputation. The fit is especially important. If the socket is too tight, too loose or incorrectly shaped, it may cause discomfort, pressure point or skin irritation.
### Above-Knee Prostheses
The medical term for this level of amputation is transfemoral amputation. The artifical knee must provide stability while standing and allow controlled movement during walking. Mechanical knees use components such as hinges friction systems and hydraulic pneumatic mechanisms to control movement. More advanced models use microprocessors.
### Prostheses for the Entire Leg
This is requires major changes to the leg and in some cases to the hip aswell. Not every person will benefit from using a full prosthesis for all activities, as it is a very limited replacement.
### Sports and Running Prostheses
Desigened for activities that place different demands on the body. A device suitable for running may not be approprite for everyday walking, and a person may need different equipment for different situations.

## 6. Artificial Joints
Not all prostheses replace an entire limb, some replace a joint.
### Artifical Hip Joints
Also called hip arthroplasty. The natural hip is a ball-and-socket joint. Hip replacements are often for people whose hip joints have been seriously damaged by arthritis or certain fractures.
### Artificial Knee Joints
Artificial knee joints replace the knee.In some operations, the back surface of the kneecap is also replaced. An artificial knee joint is implanted inside a person's existing leg, wheras a prosthetic leg replaces a missing part of the limb.
## 7. Advantages and Challanges of Prosthetic Devices
Not only does it improve independence and mobility. It can also improve a person psychologically. They still have major limitations as they are not a perfect replacement.
